# Describing Extent and Distribution

When describing a chest X-ray finding, it is useful to characterize its extent: a focal opacity is confined to a single segment or lobe, a multifocal opacity involves two or more separate, non-contiguous areas, and a diffuse opacity involves most or all of one or both lungs.

Laterality matters: unilateral findings are more typical of a localized process such as bacterial pneumonia or aspiration, while bilateral, symmetric findings raise consideration of pulmonary edema, diffuse viral pneumonia, or other systemic processes.

Distribution within the lung (upper vs. lower zone, central/perihilar vs. peripheral) can also narrow the differential — for example, lower-zone-predominant findings are common in typical bacterial pneumonia and aspiration (due to gravity-dependent regions), while upper-zone predominance raises other considerations.

These descriptive characteristics — extent, laterality, and distribution — are meant to support a structured description of what is seen, not to replace formal severity scoring systems used in clinical practice, which require correlation with vital signs, oxygen saturation, and laboratory findings that a chest X-ray alone cannot provide.
